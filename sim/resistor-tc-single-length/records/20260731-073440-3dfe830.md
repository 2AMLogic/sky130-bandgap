- **Claim**: Issue #25 -- re-measures the temperature coefficient of sky130_fd_pr__res_xhigh_po with a SINGLE-length testbench, after the two-length subtraction method in sim/resistor-flavor-characterization/ (record 20260731-044337-a8c4147) returned an essentially flat TC that disagrees with the PDK model card's static tc1 = -1.47e-3. Primary DUT is res_xhigh_po W=1um L=50um biased at 5uA (~106k, ~0.53V drop) -- a representative bias-setting-resistor operating point for this block: 5uA lies inside the 2-20uA per-branch range the <50uA Iq budget implies (design/device-characterization-summary.md sec.3) and ~0.5V is the realistic drop for such a leg off 3.3V. Three companion legs make the diagnosis falsifiable rather than merely different from the two-length run: (a) the same geometry at 10x lower bias (0.5uA) isolates any voltage-coefficient contribution; (b) the same current density (5uA/um) at 10x shorter length (L=5um) isolates both the model's length-dependent Efac voltco term and the fixed head/contact resistance's share of the total; (c) res_high_po at the identical length and bias is a positive control whose TC is already cross-validated against its model card. A fourth, raw-SPICE block probes the simulator itself: three ideal resistors sharing one .model card (tc1=-1.47e-3 tc2=2.7e-6 tnom=25) written as a literal r=1000, a constant expression r='1000*1.0', and a voltage-dependent expression r='1000*(1+1e-12*abs(v(TC_BEH,0)))'. sky130_fd_pr__res_xhigh_po implements its rbody as exactly that last element type, so if literal and constant-expression resistors track .temp while the voltage-dependent one does not, the flat measured TC is an ngspice/model-implementation artifact rather than a two-length-subtraction artifact or real PDK behaviour. Process axis mirrors the sibling experiment: the standard tt/ss/ff/sf/fs corners plus the PDK's dedicated resistor-value skew corners ll/hh, which is where poly-resistor sheet resistance actually varies (see sim/pdk.json process_corners notes). Supply axis intentionally fixed at nominal 3.30V: every DUT is biased by an ideal ground-referenced DC current source with no supply-referenced terminal, so a supply sweep cannot move any measurement here -- same rationale as the sibling experiment. This experiment resolves a measurement-methodology question only; it does not change any resistor-flavor recommendation.
